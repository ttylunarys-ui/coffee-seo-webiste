What Is Pour Over Coffee?

Pour over coffee is a manual brewing method in which hot water is poured over ground coffee held in a dripper. The water flows through the coffee bed and a filter into a cup or server. Because you control how much water you use and how quickly you pour it, you can adjust the brewing process to suit different coffee beans.

Compared with many automatic brewing methods, pour over gives you more direct control over variables such as water temperature, grind size, and pouring technique. Paper filters also tend to produce a cup with less sediment and fewer coffee oils than many metal-filter methods.

Beginner tip: Do not try to perfect every variable at once. Start with a consistent recipe, taste the result, and change one variable at a time.

What You Need to Make Pour Over Coffee

You do not need an expensive setup to get started. A few basic tools are enough to make a good cup at home.

  • Coffee dripper: Holds the filter and coffee grounds during brewing.
  • Paper filter: Traps most of the coffee grounds and helps produce a cleaner cup.
  • Fresh coffee beans: Choose beans you enjoy and store them in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight.
  • Coffee grinder: Helps you control grind size and grind the beans just before brewing.
  • Digital scale: Makes it easier to repeat the same coffee-to-water ratio.
  • Kettle: A gooseneck kettle can make controlled pouring easier, although it is not essential for beginners.

Best Pour Over Coffee Ratio and Brewing Parameters

Your brewing recipe influences how strong and balanced your coffee tastes. The following values are practical starting points for a beginner, rather than strict rules.

Variable Starting Point Why It Matters
Coffee 20 grams Sets the amount of coffee used.
Water 300 grams Creates a 1:15 coffee-to-water ratio.
Water temperature 90–96°C Affects how quickly flavor compounds dissolve.
Grind size Medium-fine Influences water flow and extraction.
Total brew time About 2.5–4 minutes Provides a useful reference for consistency.

1. Coffee-to-Water Ratio

The coffee-to-water ratio compares the weight of dry coffee with the weight of brewing water. A 1:15 ratio means one gram of coffee for every fifteen grams of water.

For example, 20 grams of coffee with 300 grams of water gives you a 1:15 ratio. You can experiment with a 1:16 ratio for a slightly lighter brew or a 1:14 ratio for a stronger one. Taste and adjust instead of assuming one ratio works for every bean.

2. Grind Size

A medium-fine grind is a useful starting point for many pour over brewers. Finer grounds generally slow water flow and increase extraction, while coarser grounds usually allow water to pass through more quickly.

If the coffee tastes harsh or drying, try a slightly coarser grind. If it tastes sour and underdeveloped, a slightly finer grind may help. These are starting adjustments, not universal diagnoses.

3. Water Temperature

Water temperature affects extraction. A starting range of 90–96°C works for many coffees, but lighter roasts may benefit from hotter water, while darker roasts can sometimes taste more balanced with cooler water.

4. Total Brewing Time

Many beginner recipes finish in roughly 2.5–4 minutes. Your dripper, filter, grind size, and pouring technique can change this time. Use the clock to help you repeat a recipe, but do not judge the coffee by time alone.

How to Make Pour Over Coffee Step by Step

Follow this simple recipe to brew one cup using 20 grams of coffee and 300 grams of water.

1

Prepare the Filter and Dripper

Place the paper filter inside your dripper. Pour some hot water through the filter to rinse it and warm the dripper and server. Discard the rinse water before adding coffee.

This step can help reduce paper taste and prevent the brewing equipment from cooling the water too quickly.

2

Weigh and Grind the Coffee

Weigh 20 grams of coffee beans and grind them to a medium-fine consistency. Add the grounds to the filter and gently level the coffee bed.

Using a scale helps you repeat the same recipe instead of guessing the amount of coffee.

3

Bloom the Coffee

Start a timer and pour about 40–60 grams of water evenly over the grounds. Wait approximately 30–45 seconds.

This stage is called the bloom. It allows trapped gases to escape and helps prepare the coffee bed for the rest of the brew.

4

Continue Pouring Slowly

Pour the remaining water in a controlled, gentle stream. Use small circular movements to wet the grounds evenly, avoiding a forceful pour directly against the filter.

Continue until your scale shows a total of 300 grams of water. Try to keep your technique consistent from one brew to the next.

5

Let the Coffee Drain and Taste

Allow the remaining water to drain through the coffee bed. Note the total brew time and taste the coffee after it has cooled slightly.

If the cup is not balanced, adjust one variable at a time. Grind size is often a useful first adjustment.

Repeatable recipe: 20 g coffee + 300 g water + a 30–45 second bloom + controlled pouring. Keep the recipe consistent before experimenting with different variables.

Common Pour Over Coffee Mistakes

Changing Too Many Variables at Once

If you change the grind size, water temperature, ratio, and pouring method together, it becomes difficult to identify what improved or worsened the taste. Change one variable at a time.

Using Inconsistent Measurements

Estimating coffee and water by eye can produce different results each time. A basic digital scale helps make your brewing more consistent.

Pouring Unevenly

Pouring too aggressively in one spot can disturb the coffee bed. Try a steady stream and distribute water across the grounds.

Ignoring Taste

A target brewing time is only a reference. Taste the coffee and use the result to guide your next adjustment.

How to Fix Bitter, Sour, or Weak Pour Over Coffee

Flavor can be affected by several variables, including extraction, roast level, water quality, and the coffee itself. Use these suggestions as a starting point.

Coffee Tastes Too Bitter

Try a slightly coarser grind or lower water temperature. Also check whether the coffee is naturally dark-roasted or strongly flavored.

Coffee Tastes Too Sour

Try a slightly finer grind or warmer water. Check whether the grounds are being wetted evenly during pouring.

Coffee Tastes Too Weak

Check the coffee-to-water ratio first. You can try using a little more coffee or reducing the amount of water.

Water Drains Too Slowly

Try a slightly coarser grind and avoid excessive agitation. Check that the filter and dripper are positioned correctly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best coffee-to-water ratio for pour over?

A 1:15 ratio is a useful starting point. For example, use 20 grams of coffee with 300 grams of water. Adjust the ratio according to your taste.

How long should pour over coffee take?

Many beginner recipes take approximately 2.5–4 minutes. The ideal time varies with the dripper, filter, grind size, and pouring technique.

Can I make pour over coffee without a gooseneck kettle?

Yes. A gooseneck kettle offers more control, but you can begin with a regular kettle if you pour carefully and consistently.

Should I use boiling water for pour over coffee?

Not necessarily. A starting range of 90–96°C works for many coffees. The best temperature depends on roast level and personal taste.

Do I need an expensive coffee grinder?

No. You can start with an affordable grinder that produces reasonably consistent grounds. More even grinding can make brewing adjustments easier.

Final Thoughts

Making pour over coffee is a process of observation and adjustment. Start with a simple recipe, measure your ingredients, and pay attention to how the coffee tastes. Over time, small changes to grind size, water temperature, and pouring technique can help you develop a brewing routine that suits your beans and preferences.

You do not need to master every technique on your first try. Consistency is the first step toward understanding what makes a better cup.
