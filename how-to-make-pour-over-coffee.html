How to Make Pour Over Coffee: A Beginner's Guide

A clear, no-fuss guide to brewing a balanced cup at home — from choosing your grind size to making that first steady pour.

By Coffee & Coffee Gear · Beginner's guide · 8 min read

You don't need a café full of equipment to make a good pour over. Start with freshly ground coffee, hot water, a dripper, and a little patience. The goal isn't to pour perfectly on your first try — it's to make a cup you enjoy and learn what changes its flavor.

This guide walks through a dependable starting recipe, then shows you how to adjust it if your coffee tastes too sour, bitter, or weak.

What is pour over coffee?

Pour over is a manual brewing method: you pour hot water over coffee grounds held in a filter, and the brewed coffee drips into a cup or carafe below. Because you control the water and the pace of the pour, it's easier to experiment with how a coffee tastes.

Compared with an automatic coffee maker, pour over asks for a little more attention. In return, you can make small adjustments instead of relying on one fixed setting.

What you'll need

You can start with a small setup. A few tools make the process easier, but you don't have to buy everything at once.

  • Coffee beans: use beans you enjoy drinking, ideally ground just before brewing.
  • Dripper and paper filter: choose a filter that fits your dripper.
  • Kettle: a gooseneck kettle gives you more control, but a regular kettle can work.
  • Digital scale: measuring coffee and water makes each attempt easier to repeat.
  • Timer and cup: a phone timer and your usual mug are enough to begin.

One useful first purchase: if you only add one tool, consider a digital scale. It helps you repeat a recipe and understand whether a change in taste came from the grind, the water, or the amount of coffee.

How to make pour over coffee, step by step

Give yourself a few minutes and don't worry about getting every movement exactly right. Even wetting and repeatable measurements matter more than fancy technique.

01

Rinse the filter and warm the dripper

Place the paper filter in the dripper and rinse it with hot water. This helps the filter sit in place and warms the brewing vessel. Pour the rinse water out before adding coffee.

02

Measure and add the coffee

Weigh out 20 grams of beans and grind them to a medium-fine texture. Add the grounds to the filter, then give the dripper a gentle shake so the coffee bed sits fairly level.

03

Bloom the grounds

Start your timer and slowly pour about 50–60 grams of water over the grounds, aiming to wet them evenly. Wait around 30–45 seconds. The coffee may swell and release bubbles; that's normal.

04

Pour slowly and steadily

Continue pouring in small circles, starting near the center and moving outward without pouring directly onto the paper filter. Add water in a controlled stream until the scale reads 300 grams in total.

Try not to rush or repeatedly splash the coffee bed. A calm, consistent pour is easier to repeat than a complicated pattern.

05

Let it drain, then taste

Wait for the water to drain through. The total brew may take around 2.5–4 minutes, depending on the coffee, grinder, and dripper. Give the coffee a gentle swirl and taste it once it's cool enough to sip.

Common mistakes to avoid

Most disappointing cups come from a handful of small issues. You don't need to fix everything at once — pick the one that seems most likely and test it on your next brew.

Changing too many things at once

If you change the grind, ratio, and temperature together, you won't know which change helped. Keep the recipe steady and adjust one variable at a time.

Pouring too quickly

A fast, uneven pour can disturb the coffee bed and make the brew less consistent. Slow down and aim to wet the grounds evenly.

Ignoring the taste because of the timer

Brew time is useful information, but it isn't the final judge. A cup that tastes good to you matters more than hitting an exact number of minutes.

Using stale or poorly stored beans

Even a careful brewing technique can't make every coffee taste fresh. Keep beans sealed away from heat and direct sunlight, and try to grind only what you need.

How to fix bitter, sour, or weak coffee

Use taste as your guide. These adjustments are starting points, not universal rules — roast level and the coffee itself can affect the result.

Too bitter or dry?

Try a slightly coarser grind or cooler water. If the beans are very dark-roasted, their natural flavor may also be contributing to the bitterness.

Sharp or sour?

Try grinding a little finer or using warmer water. Check that the water reaches all the grounds rather than running down one side.

Weak or watery?

Check your measurements first. Try a little more coffee or slightly less water, then compare the result.

Draining very slowly?

A finer grind may be slowing the flow. Try a slightly coarser setting and avoid stirring or swirling the grounds too aggressively.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best coffee-to-water ratio for pour over?

A 1:15 ratio is a useful starting point. For example, use 20 grams of coffee with 300 grams of water, then adjust to suit your taste.

How long should pour over coffee take?

Many beginner recipes take about 2.5–4 minutes. The dripper, filter, grind size, and pouring technique can all affect the time.

Can I make pour over without a gooseneck kettle?

Yes. A gooseneck kettle offers more control, but you can start with a regular kettle if you pour carefully and consistently.

Should I use boiling water?

Not always. A starting range of 90–96°C works for many coffees. Darker roasts may taste better with slightly cooler water, while lighter roasts can benefit from hotter water.

Do I need an expensive grinder?

No. Start with what you have if it produces reasonably consistent grounds. More even grinding can make it easier to control the flavor as you improve.

Make one small adjustment at a time

Your first pour over doesn't need to be perfect. Begin with the 20-gram recipe, take a note of how the cup tastes, and change one thing on the next attempt. After a few brews, you'll start to notice which adjustments make your coffee sweeter, cleaner, or fuller-bodied.

The best recipe is the one you can repeat and enjoy. Once the basics feel comfortable, experiment with different beans and find the balance you like.
