The Everyday Guide

Four Essentials for Better Coffee

Start with these basics before changing your equipment. Small adjustments to storage and brewing can help you get more consistent results.

Tip 01 · Freshness

Store Coffee Beans Properly

Coffee gradually loses its freshness when exposed to air, heat, light, and moisture. Keep your beans in an airtight container in a cool, dry cupboard, away from direct sunlight.

Buy an amount you can reasonably use while the coffee is still fresh, and avoid leaving the bag open on the counter.

Tip 02 · Water

Pay Attention to Water Temperature

Water temperature influences how quickly flavor compounds dissolve from ground coffee. For many pour-over recipes, a useful starting range is around 90–96°C (194–205°F).

Lighter roasts may benefit from hotter water, while darker roasts can sometimes taste better at a slightly lower temperature. Adjust to taste rather than treating one number as a strict rule.

Tip 03 · Grinding

Adjust Your Grind Size

Grind size affects how quickly water flows through coffee and how much flavor is extracted. A finer grind generally extracts more quickly, while a coarser grind slows extraction.

If your coffee tastes harsh or bitter, try a slightly coarser grind. If it tastes thin or sharply sour, try a slightly finer grind and compare the result.

Tip 04 · Measurement

Find a Consistent Coffee-to-Water Ratio

Measuring coffee and water makes it easier to repeat a recipe. For pour-over coffee, try starting with 1 gram of coffee for every 15–17 grams of water.

Use a kitchen scale if possible. Once you find a ratio you enjoy, keep it consistent while adjusting one other variable at a time.

A Simple Starting Point

Make Your Brewing Routine More Consistent

When a cup of coffee does not taste right, changing everything at once makes it difficult to understand the cause. Start with a repeatable recipe, then adjust one variable at a time.

Keep track of your coffee dose, water amount, grind setting, and approximate brewing time. These details help you compare one cup with the next.

A simple pour-over recipe 20 g coffee · 320 g water · approximately 1:16 ratio

Treat this as a starting point, not a fixed rule. Your coffee, dripper, and personal taste may call for adjustments.

Troubleshooting

Common Coffee Problems

Before replacing your equipment, try identifying what might be affecting the flavor of your coffee.

Coffee Tastes Too Bitter

Try a coarser grind, slightly cooler water, or a shorter extraction time. Change one variable at a time so you can tell what improves the flavor.

Coffee Tastes Too Sour

Try a finer grind or slightly hotter water. Check that your brewing process allows the water to contact the grounds evenly.

Coffee Tastes Weak

Check your coffee-to-water ratio first. Try using a little more coffee or slightly less water before changing several other parts of the recipe.
